# Explainable risk priority (Phase 5)

Original `Finding.severity` and `Finding.confidence` remain unchanged. `RiskAssessment` is a separate prioritization annotation, not CVSS and not a probability of compromise. It does not assert application exploitability, malicious intent, successful execution, theft, or runtime reachability.

Each assessment names its subject group, primary severity, propagated confidence, evidence strength, correlation strength, exposure signal, final `RiskPriority`, contributing finding fingerprints, supporting edge IDs, rationale, and limitations. The vocabulary is deliberately coarse: STRONG/MODERATE/WEAK evidence; UNKNOWN/EXTERNAL_INPUT/NETWORK_CONTEXT exposure. `EXTERNAL_INPUT` is used only when a scanner supplies structured `HTTP_INPUT` source evidence; `NETWORK_CONTEXT` records local association and is not external exposure proof.

Priority begins at the primary finding severity rank. A single-level bonus is allowed only for a LOW or MEDIUM primary finding with HIGH assessment confidence, structured `HTTP_INPUT` exposure, and direct same-sink SAST/behavior support. No bonus applies to HIGH or CRITICAL, and no multiple-edge accumulation occurs. A supporting MEDIUM shell behavior and INFO process behavior alongside one HIGH command-injection finding therefore produce one HIGH priority group, not three added severities. Contextual secret, dependency, and proximity relations do not raise priority. This conservative cap avoids numerical-score theater; no 0–100 compromise score is emitted.

Confidence falls to the minimum of direct supporting finding confidence. Incomplete scanner or correlation coverage caps assessment confidence at MEDIUM and is stated in limitations. A dependency advisory is `PRESENT` by default; a validated import reference can make it `REFERENCED`. Neither state means affected function reachability or exploitability. OSV severity/CVSS are preserved in the original dependency finding and are not redefined here.
